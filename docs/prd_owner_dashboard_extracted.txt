How to read this document
Sections 1–4 explain the why: the problem, the opportunity, goals and the people BuildTrack serves.
Sections 5–6 define who can do what (roles, permissions and dashboards).
Section 7 is the core: every functional requirement, grouped by feature area. Each requirement has an ID
(e.g. FR-PAY-03) and a target release.
Section 8 shows the key lifecycles (project, stage, payment, dispute) as states and flows.
Sections 9–14 cover the business model, non-functional requirements, integrations, release plan, risks and
open items.
Appendices hold reference material such as a worked stage preset.

v2 Next major release: pay through the app, platform-resolved disputes, paid organisation plan,
multiple contractors per project, extra roles.

can slip to a point release if needed), Could (nice to have).

2.3 Value proposition & diﬀerentiators
Diﬀerentiator Why it matters
Client-first, two-sided design The client has a real dashboard and real authority (approvals), not a guest view.
Verified progress Only client-approved stages count toward % complete; reported progress is shown
separately.
Evidence you can trust GPS- and time-stamped photos/videos against a stage checklist, with minimum
proof before review.
Payments tied to proof Stage approval makes its payment due; receipts, confirmations and disputes are
recorded with deadlines.
Verified contractors only CAC, TIN, director identity and bank-name checks before a contractor can use the
app.
Two-way reputation Clients rate contractors and contractors rate clients; unresolved disputes appear
on both profiles; NIN/passport checks stop clients escaping bad reviews.
Replaces WhatsApp for project
work
Built-in chat keeps every conversation with the project, even when staﬀ leave.
Built for Nigeria Android-friendly, oﬀline capture, naira by default, Nigerian verification
(CAC/TIN/NIN), local stage presets (DPC, lintel, decking).
Motivated crews Optional reward points turn good, verified work into higher pay.
3 Goals, non-goals & success metrics
3.1 Product goals
1. Trust: give clients a verified, evidence-based view of progress and payments on every project.
2. Adoption by contractors: make BuildTrack the easiest way for a Nigerian contractor to run projects and
win clients (especially diaspora clients).
3. Replace informal channels: move project communication, approvals and payment records out of
WhatsApp and notebooks into a structured record.
4. Fair accountability both ways: make it visible when either side behaves badly (unconfirmed payments,
repeated rejections, unresolved disputes).
5. Sustainable business: generate recurring revenue from contractor subscriptions while keeping individual
clients free.
3.2 Non-goals
Not a payment processor or escrow provider in v1 — money moves outside the app.
Not a platform-run dispute court in v1 — parties resolve disputes themselves (the platform steps in from
v2).

The Client Admin can create as many Client Rep accounts as needed and switch each module on or oﬀ per

projects, except the account-level actions reserved for the Client Admin (identity verification, deleting the
account, creating Client Reps unless granted "Invite people").

7.11 Stage review: approval, rejection & overdue reviews
Stage review is the heart of verified progress. It shows whose turn it is at every step (ball in court).
ID Requirement Priority Release
FR-REV-01 When the Site Manager (or Director) marks a stage Ready for review, the ball
moves to the client and the Client Admin and Client Reps with the "Approve /
reject stages" module are notified.
Must v1.0
FR-REV-02 Approve: the stage becomes Approved, counts toward verified progress, and any
stage payment falls due (7.13).
Must v1.0
FR-REV-03 Reject: the reviewer must give a reason by ticking failed checklist items and
adding comments, photos or voice notes. Each failed item becomes an issue
assigned to the contractor; the stage returns to In progress.
Must v1.0
FR-REV-04 The contractor fixes the issues, uploads new proof and resubmits. The full history
of every round is kept.
Must v1.0
FR-REV-05 There is no limit on rejections, but on the 3rd rejection of the same stage it is
flagged "Repeated rejection" to both sides.
Must v1.0
FR-REV-06 Either side can raise a stage dispute if they cannot agree (same process as
payment disputes, 7.15).
Must v1.0
FR-REV-07 If the client does not respond, reminders go out on day 1, 3 and 5 after
submission (see 7.18 for channels).
Must v1.0
FR-REV-08 There is no auto-approval. After 7 days the stage shows as "Awaiting client
review (overdue)". The contractor may continue to the next stage so work is not
blocked.
Must v1.0
FR-REV-09 An overdue stage does not count toward verified progress until approved, and
the waiting time is shown on the project timeline as a client-caused delay.
Must v1.0
FR-REV-10 Stages past their target end date are automatically flagged as delayed. Must v1.0
7.12 Progress calculation
BuildTrack shows two numbers side by side, and never mixes them.
ID Requirement Priority Release
FR-PCT-01 Verified progress = the sum of the weights of approved stages. This is the
headline figure.
Must v1.0
FR-PCT-02 Reported progress = the contractor's own estimate (a rough % per stage),
shown separately and clearly labelled.
Must v1.0
FR-PCT-03 Dashboards also show: stages delayed, stages overdue for review, money paid vs
remaining, open issues, and whose turn it is.
Must v1.0

7.22 Subscriptions & billing
Contractors pay; individual clients are always free. Actual prices are set in the business phase; this section
defines the model.
ID Requirement Priority Release
FR-BIL-01 Contractor pricing uses a hybrid model: tiers that each include a set number of
active projects, plus a flat fee for each extra active project above the tier's
allowance.
Must v1.0
FR-BIL-02 Unlimited users on every tier. Must v1.0
FR-BIL-03 Only active (in-progress) projects count. Completed and archived projects never
count toward the allowance and stay viewable.
Must v1.0
FR-BIL-04 Free tier: a contractor's first project is free until it is finished, up to 12
months. Any further project requires a paid plan.
Must v1.0
FR-BIL-05 Monthly and yearly billing, with a discount for yearly. Should v1.0
FR-BIL-06 Billing is in naira. When a contractor regularly exceeds its allowance, the app
suggests the tier that would be cheaper.
Should v1.0
FR-BIL-07 If a subscription lapses, active projects become read-only for the contractor
(clients keep full view of their records) until payment is made.
Should v1.0
FR-BIL-08 Organisation clients are free under Early Access in v1, limited to a small
number of active projects (e.g. 3; final number set in the business phase). The
Early Access label is shown from day one.
Must v1.0
FR-BIL-09 Paid organisation plan with portfolio dashboard and audit reports. Early Access
organisations receive founding-member perks (e.g. existing projects stay free, or
a founding discount) and at least 1–2 months' notice before charging starts.
Must v2
FR-BIL-10 Paid client add-ons such as independent site inspections. Could Later

9 Business model
Contractors pay a subscription; individual clients are always free; organisation clients are free under Early
Access in v1 and paid from v2.
Who Pays? Details
Contractors Yes — main
revenue
Hybrid tiers (included active projects + fee per extra project),
unlimited users, monthly or yearly. First project free until finished,
up to 12 months.
Individual clients Free, always Free clients bring contractors onto the platform: every client who
invites a contractor brings in a paying company.
Organisation clients Free in v1 (Early
Access); paid
from v2
Labelled Early Access from day one, limited active projects. v2 paid
plan adds portfolio dashboard and audit reports; founding-member
perks and advance notice for early users.
Client add-ons Later E.g. independent site inspection by a BuildTrack-vetted inspector.
9.1 Why this model
Industry standard: Procore and Buildertrend charge the contractor and let clients join free; unlimited
users drives adoption.
Value to contractors: the app runs their projects and wins them clients (verified badge, ratings, "track your
build live").
Fairness: charging by active projects matches usage; the per-project overage removes the "tier jump"
problem.
Avoided: per-seat pricing (discourages adding crew, who supply the proof) and percentage-of-contract-
value pricing (strongly disliked by Procore users).
Prices are not set
Actual naira prices, tier allowances, the extra-project fee, the yearly discount and the Early Access project
cap will be decided in the business phase, informed by interviews with Nigerian contractors and the
running-cost estimate from the SRD (hosting, media storage, SMS, verification checks).
