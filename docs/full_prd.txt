1 Introduction
1.1 Purpose of this document
This PRD defines what BuildTrack must do and why. It is the single source of truth for scope, user roles,
behaviour and business rules agreed during the ideation phase. It deliberately avoids implementation detail
(architecture, database design, APIs, technology choices); those belong in the Software Requirements
Document (SRD) that follows.
1.2 Product summary
BuildTrack is a two-sided construction tracking platform connecting the people who pay for a build
(Clients) with the company that builds it (the Contractor).
Contractors run their projects in BuildTrack: they set up each project from a stage preset, agree the plan
and payment structure with the client, assign their crew, and post progress with photo, video and GPS-
stamped proof.
Clients — individuals (including Nigerians in the diaspora), governments and organisations — follow their
build remotely, approve or reject each stage based on evidence, record payments, and rate the contractor.
Verified progress is the core idea: a stage only counts as complete once the client has approved it against
the proof. Everything else — payments, disputes, ratings, rewards — builds on that record.
BuildTrack is the first standalone app under the All Tracker umbrella brand. All Tracker is a family of separate,
vertical-specific tracking apps (similar to how Adobe has multiple distinct apps under one brand); SPANR (car
repair tracking) is a sibling app.

2 Problem & opportunity
2.1 Problem statement
People funding construction in Nigeria — especially from abroad — cannot see or verify what is happening
on site, and the tools they rely on today do not create trust.
No verified view of progress. Clients depend on occasional photos and the contractor's word. Stories of
stalled or abandoned builds ("stuck at lintel level") and misused funds are common, which makes
diaspora clients hesitant to build at home.
WhatsApp is the system of record. Updates, approvals and payment proof live in chats that get deleted,
lost with phones, or leave with staﬀ. Nothing is structured, dated to a stage or usable in a dispute.
Money is not tied to proof. Payments are made on trust; there is no shared record of what was paid,
against which stage, and whether it was received.
Honest contractors cannot prove they are honest. Good builders have no portable reputation or verified
track record to win clients, especially remote ones.
Existing software does not fit. Global tools are built and priced for large US/European contractors, treat
the client as a guest, and are not designed for Nigeria.
2.2 Market context
Competitor research (see References) found two groups: systems of record that run the contractor's project
(Procore) and "eyes on site" tools that turn imagery into progress (OpenSpace, Track3D, TrueLook).

3 Goals, non-goals & success metrics
3.1 Product goals
1. Trust: give clients a verified, evidence-based view of progress and payments on every project.
2. Adoption by contractors: make BuildTrack the easiest way for a Nigerian contractor to run projects and
win clients (especially diaspora clients).
3. Replace informal channels: move project communication, approvals and payment records out of
WhatsApp and notebooks into a structured record.
4. Fair accountability both ways: make it visible when either side behaves badly (unconfirmed payments,
repeated rejections, unresolved disputes).
5. Sustainable business: generate recurring revenue from contractor subscriptions while keeping individual
clients free.
3.2 Non-goals
Not a payment processor or escrow provider in v1 — money moves outside the app.
Not a platform-run dispute court in v1 — parties resolve disputes themselves (the platform steps in from
v2).

The Client Admin can create as many Client Rep accounts as needed and switch each module on or oﬀ per
Client Rep. A Client Rep with every module switched on can do everything a Client Admin can do on those
projects, except the account-level actions reserved for the Client Admin (identity verification, deleting the
account, creating Client Reps unless granted "Invite people").

create live projects, appear in the directory or receive client invitations until the Platform Admin team
approves verification. Checks are automated where a provider API exists (CAC, TIN, identity, bank account
name) and reviewed by the Platform Admin team.

9 Business model
Contractors pay a subscription; individual clients are always free; organisation clients are free under Early
Access in v1 and paid from v2.
Who Pays? Details
Contractors Yes — main
revenue
Hybrid tiers (included active projects + fee per extra project),
unlimited users, monthly or yearly. First project free until finished,
up to 12 months.
Individual clients Free, always Free clients bring contractors onto the platform: every client who
invites a contractor brings in a paying company.
Organisation clients Free in v1 (Early
Access); paid
from v2
Labelled Early Access from day one, limited active projects. v2 paid
plan adds portfolio dashboard and audit reports; founding-member
perks and advance notice for early users.
Client add-ons Later E.g. independent site inspection by a BuildTrack-vetted inspector.
9.1 Why this model
Industry standard: Procore and Buildertrend charge the contractor and let clients join free; unlimited
users drives adoption.
Value to contractors: the app runs their projects and wins them clients (verified badge, ratings, "track your
build live").
Fairness: charging by active projects matches usage; the per-project overage removes the "tier jump"
problem.
Avoided: per-seat pricing (discourages adding crew, who supply the proof) and percentage-of-contract-
value pricing (strongly disliked by Procore users).
Prices are not set
Actual naira prices, tier allowances, the extra-project fee, the yearly discount and the Early Access project
cap will be decided in the business phase, informed by interviews with Nigerian contractors and the
running-cost estimate from the SRD (hosting, media storage, SMS, verification checks).
